What if the brush were a function?

Generative Designs started as a question: what can you make when programming concepts are the paintbrush?

It’s 25 small experiments in six collections, each running live in the browser:

  • System randomness. Patterns from chance.
  • Sound. Audio turned into motion and music.
  • Programming principles. Algorithms you can watch.
  • Images. Photos rebuilt from their pixels.
  • Machine learning. On a webcam feed.
  • Miscellaneous. Noise, recursion and curves.

Every piece has controls, so you can push it and see what changes.

Running, not described

A quiet frame for loud pieces

The first version, in 2022, was a folder of flat HTML pages. In 2026 I redesigned it as a gallery:

  • A frame that stays out of the way. White, black and two greys, Space Grotesk headings, Inter body text and 4px corners. The only colour on the site lives inside the artworks.
  • A real structure. Collections are one data array rendered into cards, and every experiment page shares one layout, controls and a way back.
  • Usable controls. Sliders and buttons on every page, keyboard access, and touch support for the synth pads.
  • Rebuilt pieces. The sorting, search, FFT and synthesiser pages were rebuilt from scratch to be clearer and smoother.

Algorithms you can pause

To make a sort watchable, the draw loop has to be able to stop it halfway. So each algorithm is a generator that yields the pair it’s comparing, and every frame pulls as many steps as the speed slider asks for.

Merge, quick and heap sort use yield* for their recursive calls, and the search page uses the same pattern for its five searches.

The FFT ring groups 1024 frequency bins into 72 bands on a log scale, so it looks the way music sounds. Bars rise instantly and fall softly.

By the numbers

25 experiments in 6 collections, about 2,500 lines of sketch code, and no build step. The 2026 redesign touched about 9,000 lines across 14 commits and moved everything into a shared structure.
